NET-007 — VLAN Segmentation and Policy Enforcement Findings

Objective:
Create a second Layer-2 broadcast domain, validate inter-VLAN routing, and then enforce intentional isolation between the new VLAN and the existing lab network.

Baseline:
The existing lab LAN used VLAN 1 and subnet 10.10.20.0/24.
The lab gateway was 10.10.20.1.
Yoda remained on 10.10.20.10.
ENVY initially used 10.10.20.101.

The managed switch initially had 802.1Q disabled and all switch ports used PVID 1.

VLAN 30 Creation:
A new routed network was created on the ER605:

VLAN ID: 30
Subnet: 10.10.30.0/24
Gateway: 10.10.30.1
DHCP range: 10.10.30.100-10.10.30.199

The existing VLAN 1 / 10.10.20.0/24 network was preserved.

Switch Configuration:
The switch uplink carried VLAN 30 as tagged traffic.
The ENVY access port carried VLAN 30 as untagged traffic.
The ENVY access-port PVID was changed from 1 to 30.

VLAN 30 membership:
Port 1: tagged VLAN 30 uplink
Port 3: untagged VLAN 30 access port

Port 3 PVID: 30

DHCP Validation:
After reconnecting the Ethernet interface, ENVY received:

IPv4 address: 10.10.30.100/24
Gateway: 10.10.30.1

This demonstrated successful DHCP operation across the VLAN 30 path.

Inter-VLAN Routing Test:
Before applying an isolation policy, ENVY successfully reached Yoda at 10.10.20.10.

ENVY route:
10.10.20.10 via 10.10.30.1

ICMP result:
2 packets transmitted
2 packets received
0% packet loss

The returned packets had TTL 63, consistent with traffic crossing a Layer-3 routing boundary.

Layer-2 Neighbor Validation:
ENVY did not maintain a direct neighbor entry for Yoda at 10.10.20.10.

ENVY instead maintained a reachable Layer-2 neighbor for its local gateway at 10.10.30.1.

This demonstrated that ENVY did not resolve Yoda directly at Layer 2. Traffic destined for 10.10.20.0/24 was forwarded to the VLAN 30 gateway for Layer-3 routing.

Policy Enforcement:
An ER605 IPv4 LAN-to-LAN access-control rule was created:

Name: DENY_VLAN30_TO_LAB
Policy: Block
Service: ALL
Direction: LAN->LAN
Source Network: VLAN30
Destination Network: LAN
Effective Time: Any

Post-Policy Validation:
After the ACL was applied:

ENVY -> 10.10.30.1:
PASS
2 packets transmitted
2 packets received
0% packet loss

ENVY -> 10.10.20.10:
BLOCKED
2 packets transmitted
0 packets received
100% packet loss

The route to 10.10.20.10 still existed through 10.10.30.1.

Conclusion:
The lab demonstrated three separate networking controls:

1. VLAN segmentation created separate Layer-2 broadcast domains.
2. The router provided Layer-3 communication between those networks before policy enforcement.
3. A LAN-to-LAN ACL blocked VLAN 30 from reaching the existing lab network while preserving connectivity to the VLAN 30 gateway.

The failure after ACL deployment was therefore not caused by a missing route or failed VLAN configuration. The forwarding path remained valid, while policy intentionally prevented inter-VLAN communication.

Final State:
Yoda remained on the existing 10.10.20.0/24 network.
ENVY remained on VLAN 30 at 10.10.30.0/24.
VLAN 30 remained operational.
The VLAN30-to-LAN blocking policy remained enabled.
